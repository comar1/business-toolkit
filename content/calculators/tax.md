## What is a tax calculator?

A tax calculator estimates how much of a taxable amount goes to tax, and how
much is left over, using a flat percentage rate. It's a quick way to check
"what would I owe if the rate were X%?" before you commit to a price, an
invoice, or a filing.

**This is a simple estimate, not tax advice.** Actual tax obligations in the
Philippines depend on the type of tax (such as VAT, percentage tax, or
income tax), which bracket or threshold applies, available deductions or
exemptions, and current BIR regulations — all of which this calculator does
not account for. Always confirm your real tax obligations with an
accountant or the BIR.

## Tax calculator formula

```text
Tax due = Taxable amount × (Tax rate ÷ 100)
Net amount = Taxable amount − Tax due
```

Taxable amount is the ₱ figure you're applying tax to. Tax rate is the
percentage you enter — it's an input you control, not a rate this
calculator assumes or recommends. Tax due is the resulting peso amount owed;
net amount is what's left after subtracting that tax.

## Examples

**Example 1 — a service invoice**

A freelance graphic designer bills a client a taxable amount of ₱40,000 and
wants to see the effect of a 12% rate as an example input. Tax due is
₱40,000 × (12 ÷ 100) = **₱4,800**. Net amount is ₱40,000 − ₱4,800 =
**₱35,200**.

**Example 2 — a retail sale**

A small online shop has a taxable amount of ₱15,500 on a batch of orders and
tests an illustrative 3% rate. Tax due is ₱15,500 × (3 ÷ 100) = **₱465**.
Net amount is ₱15,500 − ₱465 = **₱15,035**.

## Choosing the right rate

The rate you enter should match whatever applies to your actual
transaction — and that depends on the tax type, your registration status,
and current rules, none of which this calculator determines for you. Treat
the output as a planning estimate only. If you're unsure which rate applies,
check with the BIR or your accountant before using the result for pricing,
invoicing, or filing decisions.
