## What is ROAS?

Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) measures how much revenue your advertising
generates for every peso you spend on it. It answers a focused question:
for every peso put into ads, how many pesos came back in sales?

ROAS is one of the fastest ways to judge whether a specific campaign,
platform, or ad set is pulling its weight — and to compare performance
across different ads without getting distracted by total spend size.

## ROAS formula

```text
ROAS = Ad spend = 0 ? 0 : Revenue from ads ÷ Ad spend
```

Revenue from ads is the total sales you can attribute to the campaign.
Ad spend is what you paid to run it. The result is shown as a ratio, like
"4.2x" — meaning ₱4.20 came back for every ₱1 spent. If ad spend is ₱0,
the calculator returns 0, since there's no spend to compare against.

## Examples

**Example 1 — an online seller running Facebook ads**

An online clothing seller spends ₱12,000 on Facebook ads over a month and
tracks ₱50,000 in sales from those ads. ROAS is ₱50,000 ÷ ₱12,000 =
**4.17x** — every peso of ad spend returned ₱4.17 in sales.

**Example 2 — a digital marketing campaign with thinner returns**

A home services business runs a ₱20,000 Google Ads campaign and generates
₱35,000 in attributed bookings. ROAS is ₱35,000 ÷ ₱20,000 = **1.75x** — a
positive return, but one worth checking carefully once cost of service and
overhead are factored in.

## ROAS vs ROI

ROAS and ROI both measure return, but they account for very different
amounts of cost. ROAS only compares revenue against **ad spend** — it
ignores cost of goods, overhead, staff time, platform fees, and everything
else it took to deliver the sale. ROI compares profit against **total
cost**, including all of that.

This gap matters: a campaign can post an impressive 5x or 6x ROAS and
still leave the business unprofitable once product cost and overhead are
subtracted. ROAS is a good first signal for how efficiently ads are
converting spend into revenue, but before calling a campaign a win, run
the numbers through the [ROI calculator](/roi-calculator) to see if it's
actually profitable.
