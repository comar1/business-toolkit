## What is a commission calculator?

A commission calculator shows how much of a sale is taken as commission, and
how much is left over for you. It's the quick way to answer a question every
seller, agent, or affiliate eventually asks: if I sell this for a certain
price, what do I actually walk away with?

This is especially useful for marketplace sellers paying a platform fee (like
Shopee- or Lazada-style commission rates), real estate agents calculating
their cut of a property sale, and affiliates or sales staff working on a
percentage-based commission.

## Commission formula

```text
Commission = Sale amount × (Commission rate ÷ 100)
You keep = Sale amount − Commission
```

Enter the **sale amount** (the full price the buyer pays) and the
**commission rate** (the percentage taken by the platform, broker, or agent).
The calculator returns two results: **Commission**, the peso amount deducted,
and **You keep**, what's left for you after that deduction.

## Examples

**Example 1 — a marketplace seller**

A seller lists an item for ₱1,500 on an online marketplace that charges a 5%
commission rate. Commission is ₱1,500 × (5 ÷ 100) = **₱75**. You keep is
₱1,500 − ₱75 = **₱1,425**.

**Example 2 — a real estate agent's commission**

A real estate agent closes a property sale worth ₱3,200,000 with an agreed
commission rate of 3%. Commission is ₱3,200,000 × (3 ÷ 100) = **₱96,000**.
You keep (the amount left for the seller after the agent's commission) is
₱3,200,000 − ₱96,000 = **₱3,104,000**.

## Commission vs profit

Knowing what you keep after commission isn't the same as knowing your
profit. Commission is deducted from the sale amount, but if you also paid to
produce or acquire what you sold, that cost still needs to come out before
you know what you actually earned. Once you have your "You keep" figure from
this calculator, run it through the
[profit margin calculator](/profit-margin-calculator) against your cost of
goods to see what's really left after commissions.
