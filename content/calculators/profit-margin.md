## What is profit margin?

Profit margin is the percentage of your revenue that remains as profit after
subtracting the cost of what you sold. It answers a simple but important
question: for every peso that comes in, how much do you actually keep?

Margin is the most common way to judge whether a price is sustainable,
compare performance across products, and spot when rising costs are quietly
eating into what you take home.

## Profit margin formula

```text
Profit = Revenue − Cost
Margin (%) = (Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100
```

Revenue is the total amount a sale brought in. Cost is what it cost you to
deliver that sale (cost of goods sold). The result is a percentage: the
share of every peso of revenue that is pure profit.

## Examples

**Example 1 — a small retail sale**

A sari-sari store sells ₱100,000 worth of goods in a month, with ₱70,000 in
cost of goods. Profit is ₱30,000, and the margin is (₱30,000 ÷ ₱100,000) ×
100 = **30%**.

**Example 2 — a tight-margin service**

A delivery service bills a client ₱25,000 for a job that cost ₱22,000 to
fulfil (fuel, driver pay, packaging). Profit is ₱3,000, giving a margin of
(₱3,000 ÷ ₱25,000) × 100 = **12%** — a reminder that revenue size alone
doesn't tell you how healthy a business is.

## Margin vs markup

Margin and markup both measure profit, but from different starting points:
margin is profit over **revenue**, markup is profit over **cost**. They will
always give different percentages on the same sale (markup is always the
larger number when there's a profit). If you're pricing a product starting
from its cost, the [markup calculator](/markup-calculator) is usually the
more natural starting point; margin is better for checking how a final
price performs.
