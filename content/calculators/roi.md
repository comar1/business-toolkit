## What is ROI?

Return on investment (ROI) measures how much profit an investment generated
compared to what it cost. It answers a direct question: for every peso you
put in, how much did you get back — and was it actually worth doing?

ROI is one of the most widely used numbers in business because it works for
almost any kind of spending decision: buying equipment, stocking inventory,
running a marketing campaign, or taking on a new project. It strips the
decision down to a single percentage you can compare across very different
choices.

## ROI formula

```text
Net gain = Total return − Investment cost
ROI (%) = (Net gain ÷ Investment cost) × 100
```

Total return is the full amount the investment brought back. Investment
cost is what you put in to make it happen. If investment cost is zero, ROI
is reported as 0% since there's nothing to divide against.

## Examples

**Example 1 — buying new equipment**

A small bakery spends ₱80,000 on a new commercial oven (investment cost).
Over the following year, the oven lets the bakery produce more and sell an
extra ₱104,000 worth of baked goods (total return). Net gain is ₱104,000 −
₱80,000 = ₱24,000, and ROI is (₱24,000 ÷ ₱80,000) × 100 = **30%** — the
oven paid for itself and then some.

**Example 2 — a marketing campaign that underperformed**

A clothing shop spends ₱50,000 on a social media ad campaign (investment
cost) and tracks ₱38,000 in resulting sales (total return). Net gain is
₱38,000 − ₱50,000 = **−₱12,000**, and ROI is (−₱12,000 ÷ ₱50,000) × 100 =
**−24%**. The campaign lost money, which is a clear signal to revisit the
targeting, creative, or offer before spending more.

## ROI vs ROAS

ROI and ROAS both measure return, but they answer different questions.
ROAS (return on ad spend) only compares revenue against how much you spent
on ads — it ignores the cost of the product you sold, fulfillment, and any
other expenses tied to the sale. That makes ROAS useful for judging whether
an ad itself is efficient, but it can look healthy even when the underlying
activity loses money overall.

ROI accounts for your total investment cost, not just ad spend, so it tells
you whether something was actually worth doing once every cost is factored
in. If you're deciding whether to keep investing in a campaign, project, or
purchase, ROI is the better measure to trust. For a pure ad-efficiency view,
use the [ROAS calculator](/roas-calculator) alongside it.
