## What is a discount calculation?

A discount calculation tells you the final price a customer pays after a
percentage is taken off the original price, and how many pesos that
percentage actually represents. It's the quickest way to check a sale price
before you post it, print a tag, or tell a customer what they'll pay.

Whether you're running a seasonal promo, clearing out slow-moving stock, or
quoting a bulk-order discount, this calculator turns a percentage into real
peso figures so there's no guessing at the register.

## Discount formula

```text
Discount amount = Original price × (Discount ÷ 100)
Final price = Original price − Discount amount
```

Original price is what the item or service normally sells for. Discount is
the percentage being taken off. The calculator shows both the **final
price** the customer pays and **you save**, the peso amount the discount
removed from the original price.

## Examples

**Example 1 — a weekend sale**

A clothing shop runs a 20% off promo on a shirt originally priced at ₱850.
The discount amount is ₱850 × (20 ÷ 100) = ₱170, so the customer pays a
final price of ₱850 − ₱170 = **₱680**, and you save shows **₱170**.

**Example 2 — a loyalty discount**

A hardware store offers a regular customer 15% off a ₱1,250 tool purchase.
The discount amount is ₱1,250 × (15 ÷ 100) = ₱187.50, bringing the final
price to ₱1,250 − ₱187.50 = **₱1,062.50**, with you save showing
**₱187.50**.

## Discounts and your margin

A discount lowers what the customer pays, but it doesn't lower what the
item cost you — so every peso of discount is a peso that used to be
profit. A 20% discount can look like a small, routine promo, but it can
quietly turn a healthy margin into a thin one or even a loss if your
original price wasn't priced with room for it. Before running a sale,
it's worth checking the final price here, then using the
[profit margin calculator](/profit-margin-calculator) to see what margin
you're left with at that price.
